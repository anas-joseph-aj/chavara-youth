Who We Are

Chavara Youth of Kristu Jayanti Deemed to be University, Bengaluru, under the aegis of the Jayatian Catholic Association (JCA), is a group of dynamic and spirited young individuals who focus on sculpting youth for the holistic development of its members in tandem with the religious and social causes of the Church. For this mission, it makes use of fellowship gatherings, praise and worship, community development activities, leadership training programmes, personality and career development activities, etc., utilising the media and technology platforms. Various faith formation modules help the members realise their innate talents, rediscover and live their faith, and be effective missionaries.

A life of faith and service

Our Founder

St. Kuriakose Elias Chavara — An Inspirational Figure for Youth and Love of the Church

St. Kuriakose Elias Chavara was a 19th-century Indian Catholic priest and visionary leader who founded the Carmelites of Mary Immaculate (CMI), the congregation that runs Kristu Jayanti Deemed to be University, Bengaluru. He was a champion of education, a social reformer, and a holy man deeply rooted in Catholic spirituality.

Early Life and Education

Born on February 10, 1805, in Kainakary, Kerala, India, Kuriakose Elias Chavara was raised in a devout Syro-Malabar Catholic family. From a young age, he demonstrated exceptional intelligence and a strong spiritual inclination. He received an education in traditional Kerala schools and later in a seminary. He was ordained as a priest in 1829.

Founding the CMI Congregation

Chavara was a 19th-century Indian Catholic priest and visionary leader. Along with Fr. Thomas Palackal, Fr. Thomas Porukkara, and Bro. Yakob Kaniyanthara, he founded the Carmelites of Mary Immaculate (CMI), which runs Kristu Jayanti Deemed to be University, Bengaluru. CMI was India's first indigenous congregation for men.

Contributions to Education and the Church

His contributions to education, spirituality, and social reform in Kerala include the first indigenous religious congregation for men (CMI), the first Sanskrit school, the first Catholic printing press, and the first Indian congregation for women (CMC).

He edited and published the East Syrian Breviary and prepared the first liturgical calendar in the Malabar Church in 1862. Through his efforts, Syriac was printed in Kerala for the first time, and the first Malayalam prayer books were published at Mannanam under his care.

A pioneer of popular education in Kerala, he advocated for schools in every parish alongside churches, inspiring the Malayalam term â€œPallikkoodamâ€—a place for education attached to the church. He promoted modern educational principles and encouraged girls' education.

Social Reform and Literary Contributions

Chavara opposed caste discrimination and alcoholism. He worked to uplift marginalized communities and promote social equality.

A prolific writer, he contributed to Malayalam literature through prayer books and spiritual writings.

Canonization and Legacy

St. Kuriakose Elias Chavara died on January 3, 1871. Pope John Paul II beatified him in 1986, and Pope Francis canonized him in 2014.

He continues to be a model of holiness, education, social justice, and spirituality. His example encourages young people to value education, inclusivity, social responsibility, spiritual growth, and love for the Church.
